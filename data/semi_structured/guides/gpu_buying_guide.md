# Buying Guide: Choosing a GPU

## Understand Your Use Case First

- **Gaming at 1080p:** A mid-range GPU is generally sufficient for high frame rates at this resolution.
- **Gaming at 1440p or 4K:** Prioritize higher VRAM (8GB+) and a stronger core/boost clock.
- **Content creation / rendering:** Prioritize VRAM capacity and CUDA/compute core count over raw gaming benchmarks.

## Key Specifications to Compare

- **VRAM (memory):** More VRAM helps with higher resolutions, texture-heavy games, and multitasking (streaming while gaming, for example).
- **Core clock / boost clock:** Higher clock speeds generally mean better raw performance within the same GPU generation.
- **Power draw (TDP):** Check that your power supply unit (PSU) can support the card's rated power draw, plus headroom for the rest of the system.
- **Physical size:** Confirm the card's length and width fit your case before purchasing.

## Budget vs. Performance

A higher price does not always mean proportionally higher performance — price-to-performance ratio often peaks in the mid-range tier of each GPU generation. If budget is a primary constraint, compare price per frame rather than raw specifications alone.

## Compatibility Checklist

Before finalizing a purchase, verify:
- The card fits physically inside your case.
- Your power supply has the required connectors and sufficient wattage.
- Your motherboard has a free PCIe slot of the appropriate generation.
